What the blended metric shows
- Index covers 2026-08-02T19:40:02Z to 2026-08-03T01:39:58Z, 7200 docs, exactly 200 docs in every 10-minute bucket (36 buckets, single shard/no replicas — no shard-merge percentile-approximation issue).
- The 01:00–01:10 bucket: doc_count 200, avg latency 455.6ms, p50 369.6ms, p90 904.1ms, p99 1798.3ms.
- Baseline buckets: avg ~100–150ms, p50 tightly clustered ~64–88ms, p90 ~170–330ms, p99 noisy but ~480–1800ms.
- The standout anomaly isn't really p99 (baseline already touches ~1715ms in one bucket) — it's p50, which jumps 4–5x above every other bucket. That pointed away from "a few slow outliers skewed the tail" and toward "the whole distribution in this bucket is different."

Root cause found by segmenting on `endpoint`
- Baseline (7000 docs, excl. spike bucket) endpoint mix: /api/cart 2151, /api/profile 2151, /api/search 2051, /api/checkout 647 (~9.2% of traffic).
- Spike bucket (200 docs) endpoint mix: /api/checkout 172 (86% of traffic), /api/cart 14, /api/profile 8, /api/search 6.
- /api/checkout is structurally the slow endpoint everywhere: baseline p50/p90/p99 = 452 / 955 / 1815ms, vs. cart/profile/search baseline p50 ≈ 68–70ms, p90 ≈ 149–150ms.
- Checkout's own latency distribution *inside* the spike bucket: p50/p90/p99 = 421 / 951 / 1844ms (n=172) — statistically the same as its own baseline (452 / 955 / 1815ms, n=647), if anything marginally faster at the median. Cart/profile/search also show no degradation in the spike bucket (small-n but in line with or better than baseline).
- Checkout call volume by 10-min bucket over the full timeline runs 12–27/bucket everywhere except 01:00–01:10, where it hits 172 — an isolated ~7–9x burst that reverts to 16 in the very next bucket (01:10–01:20). Total request volume stayed flat at exactly 200 in every bucket including this one, so it's a pure mix shift, not a traffic surge overall.
- Sampled raw docs from the burst are distinct documents (different _id, timestamps seconds apart, different regions/latencies) — not duplicated/replayed records.
- Error rate (status 500) in the spike bucket is 4/200 = 2.0%, matching the baseline rate of 142/7000 = 2.0%. No elevated failures/retries accompanying the burst.

Conclusion
No endpoint got slower. Checkout performed within its normal envelope even while absorbing ~9x its usual call volume in that window, and the light endpoints stayed fast. What happened is that for one 10-minute window, an unusually large share of the (flat-rate) traffic happened to be checkout calls — which are inherently ~6x slower than the other endpoints by design (heavier operation: payment/inventory work) — rather than the usual cart/profile/search mix. Blending all endpoints into a single latency_ms percentile with no segmentation by endpoint means that percentile is a function of the traffic mix as much as of system performance. When the mix swung toward the naturally-slow endpoint, the blended p99 swung with it even though every endpoint's own performance was unchanged. That is a measurement/aggregation artifact (Simpson's-paradox-style population shift), not a system regression — there is no evidence of queueing, saturation, resource contention, or error-rate increase anywhere in the data.

Recommendation for the on-call engineer: re-cut the p99 SLO/alert per endpoint (or at least exclude/segment /api/checkout) rather than on the blended `latency_ms` across all endpoints, and optionally look upstream for why checkout's call share spiked ~9x for that one window (e.g. a batch job, promo, or client retry pattern) — that volume shift is real and may be worth understanding, but it did not cause any latency degradation.
