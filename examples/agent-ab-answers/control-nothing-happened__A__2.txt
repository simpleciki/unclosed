Checked app-logs-000042 against OpenSearch at 127.0.0.1:9250. The index holds exactly the last ~6 hours (2026-08-02T20:10:02Z to 2026-08-03T02:09:58Z, 7200 docs, single service "storefront"), so that whole index is both the "spike" candidate and its own baseline.

What I found:

- Average latency is flat the entire window: 5-minute bucket averages range 82-108ms with no trend; even at 1-minute resolution (n=14-26/bucket) averages range 61-145ms scattered randomly across all 6 hours, mean 95.6ms throughout.
- First half (20:10-23:10, n=3600) vs second half (23:10-02:10, n=3600) are statistically indistinguishable: p50 79.5 vs 79.1, p90 173.6 vs 170.2, p99 324.6 vs 339.0, avg 96.3 vs 94.9.
- The 30 events over 400ms are spread evenly across all thirteen 30-minute buckets spanning the full 6 hours (1-4 each) — no clustering anywhere, i.e. no incident window.
- No dimension isolates a change: 4 endpoints all similar (p99 300-383ms, avg ~95-97ms), 3 regions all similar (p99 317-344ms), and the 146 HTTP 500s (2% of traffic) actually have slightly lower latency than the 200s (p99 284 vs 329) — errors aren't the slow ones.
- The latency histogram is one smooth heavy right tail (lognormal-ish: p50=79, p90=172, p99=328, p99.9=491, max=608) — no second "slow" population appeared.

Why p99 looked spiky: p99 was being read off small windows (~100-300 requests per 5-15 min bucket). At that sample size, p99 is estimated from roughly the top 1-3 requests in the bucket, so it swings widely (226ms to 501ms bucket-to-bucket in the 5-min series) purely from sampling noise on an inherently heavy-tailed distribution — even though the distribution generating those requests never changed. That's what triggered the page: a noisy statistic on a small window, not a shift in the service.

Nothing to remediate on the service side. If this keeps paging, the fix is on the alert (widen the window / raise the sample count feeding the percentile, or alert on a less noisy percentile like p90), not on app-logs-000042's traffic.
