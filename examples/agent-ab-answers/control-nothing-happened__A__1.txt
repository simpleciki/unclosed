Checked app-logs-000042 directly in OpenSearch (7,200 docs, full range 2026-08-02T20:10:02Z to 2026-08-03T02:09:58.804Z — exactly the six-hour window in question, so "baseline" and "spike" both live inside this one index).

What I found:

1. Throughput is dead flat: exactly 200 docs every 10-minute bucket for all 36 buckets, all 6 hours. No traffic drop, no burst, no ingestion gap.

2. First half (20:10-23:10) vs second half (23:10-02:10) latency_ms distributions are essentially identical: p50 79.5 vs 79.1, p90 173.6 vs 170.2, p95 215.6 vs 209.1, p99 324.6 vs 339.0, p99.9 493.5 vs 488.2. No regime change anywhere in the distribution.

3. Hourly breakdown shows the "rise" is confined to p99 only, and even that is marginal:
   avg:  97.1  95.6  95.2  93.5  96.1  98.0  86.6
   p50:  80.2  76.0  79.7  77.3  81.0  80.6  73.9
   p90: 176.9 175.6 168.4 167.2 173.2 180.2 146.0
   p99: 318.6 329.1 310.5 339.0 328.3 361.9 374.0
   The last two hours show p99 nudging up (328→362→374) while avg, p50, and p90 for those same hours are FLAT TO LOWER (the final hour has the lowest median and mean of the whole window). A genuine slowdown moves the whole distribution together; here only the single most extreme statistic wiggles, and it wiggles the wrong way relative to the rest of the distribution.

4. At finer time resolution the "spike" dissolves into noise. 5-minute buckets (n≈90-100 each) range from p99=226.8 up to p99=501.2, and the high buckets are scattered randomly across the entire 6 hours (23:20, 21:25, 01:00, 22:30, 21:35, 21:55, 20:15, 20:40...) — not clustered in any single incident window. At 1-minute resolution (n≈15-30), p99 is mathematically almost the same as "max of the bucket," so any one unlucky slow request makes that minute look like a spike (e.g. 23:23 shows p99=538 vs 240/190/148 in the surrounding minutes — that's one single 538ms request, not an incident).

5. The top 30 slowest individual requests in the whole index are spread uniformly across the full 6-hour span (20:18 through 02:09) and across all 4 endpoints and all 3 regions, with no time, endpoint, or region concentration. status=200 in 29 of 30.

6. No confound found: service is a single constant value ("storefront"); endpoint (4) and region (3) mixes are stable in proportion throughout; status=500 rate is flat at ~1.2-3.0% every 30 minutes with no spike and no elevated latency (500s are not slower than 200s); ingested_at trails @timestamp by a small, stable 0.5-4s throughout, so this isn't a delayed-ingestion/backfill artifact either.

Bottom line: nothing about the system's behavior changed in the last six hours — throughput, median, p90, error rate, and endpoint/region mix are all flat. The only thing that moves is p99, and it moves because p99 is an extreme-tail order statistic computed over a fairly small sample per time slice; with that few points in the tail, a couple of ordinary random slow requests (this app already regularly throws 400-600ms outliers all night, e.g. 607.8ms at 01:30, 564.8ms at 21:55, 561.3ms at 20:18 — all long before "recently") are enough to make whichever bucket happens to contain them look like a spike. Scan enough small buckets across 6 hours and one will always look tall. That's a property of how p99 was measured (small-N tail statistic, multiple-bucket scanning), not a property of the system.
