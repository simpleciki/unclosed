WHAT THE COMPOSITION SHIFT ACTUALLY IS
In the focus window, /api/checkout made up 172 of 200 requests (86%). Everywhere else in the index it's 647 of 7000 (9.2%) -- cart, profile, and search (2151, 2151, 2051) carry most of the normal traffic and almost none of the focus window (14, 8, 6). Region composition (78/68/54 focus vs 2381/2310/2309 baseline) and status composition (196 status-200 / 4 status-500 in focus vs 6858 status-200 / 142 status-500 in baseline) show no comparable shift, and service was 100% storefront throughout. This is specifically an endpoint-mix event, isolated to /api/checkout.

Checkout's own latency didn't move. Focus window: checkout p50 421.35, p99 1843.83, n=172. Baseline: checkout p50 452.06, p99 1815.41, n=647. Indistinguishable. What changed was how much of the window's traffic was checkout, not how fast checkout was served -- checkout has always been the slow endpoint here, so a window that's 86% checkout reads as slow overall even though nothing got slower.

The 86% share is a real, isolated anomaly, not noise: across all 36 ten-minute buckets in the lookback (2026-08-02T19:40 through 2026-08-03T01:30), every other bucket's checkout count falls between 12 and 27 out of 200. The focus bucket's 172 stands completely alone. It held for the full ten minutes rather than one sub-minute burst, and it starts and stops right at the window's boundaries -- the bucket immediately before shows checkout at 27, the one immediately after at 16.

One scale check worth keeping in view: p99 among ordinary-mix buckets already ranges from 480.91 to 1714.96 across this same lookback, purely from which few checkout requests happen to land in the tail at n=200 per bucket. The focus bucket's 1798.27 is high, but it's not off in a separate regime from what this metric already produces without any mix anomaly -- it's the composition shift, not an unprecedented number, that the report attributes the rise to.

If that's worth chasing, it needs a different data source than this one.
