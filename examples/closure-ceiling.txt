==============================================================================
unclosed -- the closure ceiling, and what was under it
==============================================================================

The question: can an assembled traversal close at all?

For a long time the answer was unknown rather than no, which is worse. Three
branches -- a deploy landed, a dependency slowed, the host lost resources -- were
appended NOT_VISITED unconditionally, and any unwalked branch keeps a chain open.
So the closure verdict was a constant. A refusal that can never be lifted is not
a strict finding; it is an absent one, and a gate that never opens is a wall.

Everything below is one pre-registered corpus -- shape, companions, seeds and the
control fixed before the first run and not adjusted after seeing any of it.

    primary index   36 buckets x 10m x 500 docs; 4 endpoints; 3 services and 3
                    regions assigned independently of endpoint; 10% of requests
                    fail. The focus bucket raises /api/checkout only.
    companions      change events (deploys, none inside the focus bucket), a
                    dependency latency series, a node CPU series -- both series
                    flat, so those branches must come back RULED_OUT and not
                    CONFIRMED.
    report          external, filed at the end of the window.
    control         the same primary index with no companions pointed at. It
                    must NOT close. If both close the companions changed
                    nothing and the run is void.

Volume is what makes status comparable here, not the error rate: 10% of 500 is
above the probe's floor of 30 where 2% of 200 was not. Raising traffic is a
different system; raising the failure rate to clear a floor would be tuning the
corpus against its own grader.

STAGE 1 -- the branches nothing could answer
--------------------------------------------

Pointed at a source, those three are now walked; left alone they stay unwalked.
That half works, and the control is the proof: with companions the tree has zero
unwalked branches, without them it has three.

The chain still did not close. 0 of 11 runs, across three planted effect sizes.
So the story "it cannot close because the data is not there" was wrong.

STAGE 2 -- what was actually stopping it
-----------------------------------------

A concentration verdict had three bands:

    CONFIRMED     p <= 0.05                 stands out from the null
    RULED_OUT     excess <= null_median     at or below the middle of it
    INCONCLUSIVE  everything between        elevated, not established

Closure needs every branch DISPOSED, and INCONCLUSIVE is not. So a chain closed
only if every *other* declared dimension landed in the lower half of its own
null -- and for a dimension with nothing concentrated in it, whose excess is a
draw from that very null, that is a coin flip by definition.

    P(close) ~ 0.5^3 = 12.5%   on data with no ambiguity in it at all

Measured it was worse, because a real rise leaks into the other dimensions: a
subgroup holding a few more of the raised requests carries a higher median.
Across 33 innocent-dimension outcomes, 12 came back RULED_OUT and 21 came back
INCONCLUSIVE -- 36%, not 50%.

    P(close) ~ 0.36^3 = 4.7%   -> 0.5 closures expected in 11 runs; 0 observed

The leak is visible directly: dropping the planted effect from a median of 900ms
to 160ms moved region from INCONCLUSIVE in 3 of 5 runs to 0 of 3, and did not
move service, which was INCONCLUSIVE in 8 of 8 at every effect size. Leakage is
part of it. The coin flip is the rest.

This is the honest-completeness principle billing itself. Every hypothesis added
to the declared catalog is another coin that has to land the same way, so naming
more of them -- what this skill exists to argue for -- made closure geometrically
less likely.

STAGE 3 -- the fix considered and rejected
-------------------------------------------

The obvious move is to redefine RULED_OUT as "not distinguishable from the null"
(p > alpha), so an innocent dimension clears 95% of the time instead of 50%.
Measured, with the definition swapped locally and reverted:

    RULED_OUT = p > alpha      900ms, 5 seeds -> CLOSED 3/5   control 0/5
                               160ms, 3 seeds -> CLOSED 1/3   control 0/3
                               ---------------------------------------------
                               4 of 8

Rejected, for two reasons and the second is the real one. The algebra had said
about 86% and it measured 50% -- reasoning where a measurement was available, in
a project whose whole argument is that those differ. And it clears a branch
*because it failed to knock it down*, which is the exact move Gate 1 refuses in
as many words: failing to knock something down is not the same as clearing it.
A tool that breaks its own rule to produce a satisfying output has produced
nothing.

STAGE 4 -- judging a branch against the effect, not only against chance
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The null answers *is this larger than noise*. Closure asks *is any rival account
still standing*, which is a question about size. Nothing connected them.

On these runs a subgroup gap of 13.5ms sat in the upper half of its own null
while the effect being explained was 1900ms. Judged against chance: elevated.
Judged against the thing it would have to account for: 0.7% of it. No confidence
about a 0.7% wobble makes it a rival account of a 1900ms rise.

IMMATERIAL is the state for a branch that was measured and is too small to be the
answer. The bar is Gate 3's own RESIDUAL_TOLERANCE, widened the same way when the
estimators disagree -- not a new constant, and deliberately the same one used to
accept an explanation, so nothing can be too small to be an answer and large
enough to block one.

    with IMMATERIAL            900ms, 5 seeds -> CLOSED 4/5   control 0/5
    (RULED_OUT unchanged)      300ms, 3 seeds -> CLOSED 2/3   control 0/3
                               160ms, 3 seeds -> CLOSED 1/3   control 0/3
                               ---------------------------------------------
                               7 of 11 closed, 0 of 11 controls

Better than the rejected fix, and it contradicts nothing: the branch is not
cleared, the tree still reports it as elevated, and only the closure gate stops
treating a 0.7% wobble as a live rival.

The rule that keeps this from being an escape hatch: a branch nobody could
measure never becomes IMMATERIAL. Too small to matter is a finding, not
something to say when you did not look. A dimension with one value present, or
with too few documents on both sides, stays INCONCLUSIVE and still blocks.

WHAT STILL DOES NOT CLOSE
--------------------------

All four remaining failures are magnitude, not concentration -- which is Gate 3
doing its job rather than a leftover of this one.

  seed 4102 at 160ms   UNDER_ACCOUNTED: +81.58 of +114.75. The confirmed
                       concentration covers 71% of the effect, outside the 20%
                       this project will size an explanation to. That chain
                       should not close.

  seed 4103, all three NOT_QUANTIFIED, with the sizes agreeing to within 1%. A
                       confirmed *explanatory* branch carried no number, which
                       short-circuits the arithmetic. The likely one is a
                       companion: an event index can report that a deploy landed
                       and cannot report how many milliseconds it explains, so
                       those nodes confirm with no magnitude by construction.
                       So a run where a deploy really did land inside the window
                       still cannot close.

                       Left as it stands, because it is not obviously wrong: a
                       strong lead you cannot size is an open chain, and that is
                       what this tool is named after. Stated as a candidate and
                       not a finding -- a concentration node confirming while its
                       removal left nothing to measure would produce the same
                       verdict, and the two were not separated.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW
------------------------

That real incidents should close. They mostly should not, and the shipping
corpus says so for a better reason: on a request-log index the hypotheses that
would explain a rise are usually not in the data at all. This measures only
whether the criteria are reachable when the data is there.
