==============================================================================
unclosed -- the closure ceiling
==============================================================================

The question this answers: can an assembled traversal close at all?

Until now the answer was unknown rather than no. Three branches -- a deploy
landed, a dependency slowed, the host lost resources -- were appended
NOT_VISITED unconditionally, and any unwalked branch keeps a chain open. So the
closure verdict was a constant, and a refusal that can never be lifted reports
nothing. Gate 1's own rule turned on the gate that owns it.

Those three are now walked when the caller points at a source for them, and the
runs below confirm that half works: with companions pointed at, unwalked
branches go to zero; in the control, the same three are still unwalked.

The chain still did not close. 0 of 11 runs, across three effect sizes. What
stops it is not missing data, and it is not bad luck.

WHAT ACTUALLY STOPS IT
----------------------

A concentration verdict has three bands:

    CONFIRMED     p <= 0.05                 stands out from the null
    RULED_OUT     excess <= null_median     at or below the middle of it
    INCONCLUSIVE  everything between        elevated, not established

Closure requires every branch to be DISPOSED, and INCONCLUSIVE is not disposed.
So a chain closes only if every *other* declared dimension lands in the lower
half of its own null.

For a dimension with nothing concentrated in it, the observed excess is a draw
from that null. Landing at or below its median is therefore roughly a coin
flip -- by definition, not by accident. With three other dimensions:

    P(close) ~ 0.5^3 = 12.5%   on data with no ambiguity in it at all

Measured here it is worse, because a real concentrated rise leaks into the other
dimensions: a subgroup that happens to hold a few more of the raised requests
carries a higher median. Across 33 innocent-dimension outcomes in the runs
below, 12 came back RULED_OUT and 21 came back INCONCLUSIVE -- 36%, not 50%.

    P(close) ~ 0.36^3 = 4.7%   -> 0.5 closures expected in 11 runs; 0 observed

The leak is visible directly in the sweep. Dropping the planted effect from a
median of 900ms to 160ms moved `region` from INCONCLUSIVE in 3 of 5 runs to 0 of
3. It did not move `service`, which was INCONCLUSIVE in 8 of 8 runs at every
effect size -- so leakage is part of the story and not all of it. The rest is
the coin flip.

THE PART THAT IS A DESIGN STATEMENT, NOT A BUG
----------------------------------------------

Naming a hypothesis this data cannot settle is the central claim of this skill:
a tree that omits its unwalked branches looks complete, and looking complete is
the failure being attacked. But every declared dimension is another coin that
has to land the same way, so **each hypothesis added halves the chance of ever
closing a chain.** Honest completeness and the closure criterion pull in
opposite directions, and the current resolution is that nothing closes.

That is not a defect in any one line of code. It is a choice about what
RULED_OUT should mean: "at the middle of the null" (what it means now, so an
innocent dimension clears half the time) or "not distinguishable from the null"
(p > alpha, so an innocent dimension clears 95% of the time, and closure with
three others goes from 12.5% to 86%). The second keeps all three reporting bands
and changes only which of them blocks a chain. Neither is written here, because
picking one is a decision about what this tool is for, not a defect to repair.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SHOW
-----------------------

That real incidents should close. They mostly should not, and the shipping
corpus says so for a different and better reason: on a request-log index the
hypotheses that would explain a rise are usually not in the data. This measures
only whether the criteria are reachable when the data *is* there.

The runs below were pre-registered -- corpus shape, companion shape, seed count
and the control were fixed before the first run and not adjusted afterwards.

==============================================================================
RUN -- planted effect: median 900ms, 5 seeds
==============================================================================
before the first run, and not reshaped afterwards:

  primary index   36 buckets x 10m x 500 docs; 4 endpoints; 3 services and 3
                  regions assigned independently of endpoint; 10% of requests
                  fail. The focus bucket raises `/api/checkout` only.
  companions      change events (deploys, none inside the focus bucket),
                  a dependency latency series, a node CPU series -- both series
                  flat, so the branches must come back RULED_OUT rather than
                  CONFIRMED.
  report          external, filed at the end of the window.
  runs            5 independent seeds.
  control         the same primary index with no companions pointed at. It must
                  NOT close. If both close, the companions changed nothing and
                  the experiment is void.

What this does and does not test. It does not test whether real incidents
usually close -- they do not, and the corpus that ships says so. It tests
whether the closure criteria are *satisfiable end to end* by an assembled
traversal, which until now was unknown: three branches were appended
NOT_VISITED unconditionally, so the closure verdict was a constant.

Volume is what makes `status` comparable here, not the error rate: 10% of 500
is above the probe's floor of 30 where 2% of 200 was not. Raising traffic is a
different system; raising the failure rate to clear a floor would be tuning the
corpus against its own grader.
