3. CONCENTRATION DETECTION FLOOR  (known limit A, measured)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The probe confirms a concentration when one value has moved further from its
  own level outside the window than the rest have moved from theirs, by more
  than redrawing the window with every group held at one level produces -- at a
  declared 5% rate. Each rung below is one strength of planted concentration,
  sized as the target's median excess over the rest as a share of the window's
  own median rise. That share is a property of the generated data, not a
  threshold the probe uses -- the constant it used to be is what this replaced.
  The first rung plants nothing.

  rung             true share    confirmed     rate   note
  x010                  14.4%          0/5       0%   below the floor: a real concentration, never established
  x012                  23.8%          1/5      20%   partial: detection is not yet reliable at this strength
  x014                  46.8%          2/5      40%   partial: detection is not yet reliable at this strength
  x016                  68.3%          5/5     100%   
  x018                  87.8%          5/5     100%   
  x020                 108.9%          5/5     100%   
  x030                 203.5%          5/5     100%   

  FLOOR: reliable detection begins at a true share of 68.3% of the
  window's own median rise. Below that the probe reports 'elevated, not
  established' and the concentration is missed.

  DIRECTION: NOT one-sided. 3 run(s) confirmed a concentration where none
  was planted. Section 2 names which cases; the risks below explain both
  sources, and they are different -- only one of them is the declared rate.

  WHAT THIS REPLACED, TWICE. The probe first required the excess to reach 50% of
  the window's own median rise. That constant failed in both directions at once,
  and this harness measured both: it found a 46.8% concentration in 2 runs of 5,
  and -- because a window whose median moved only by noise still has a median
  rise above zero -- it confirmed a concentration on the negative control in 3
  runs of 5. A null fixed that: the control went to 5 of 5 and the 46.8% rung to
  4 of 5.

  The null was then wrong in a second way, and this harness measured that too.
  It shuffled the group labels, which asks whether a gap is larger than chance
  produces and never whether the gap is NEW -- so a permanently slower subgroup
  was confirmed on `real-uniform-rise-on-skewed-index` in 5 runs of 5, where
  nothing was concentrated at all. Two changes answer it, and both are in the
  measurements above:

    each group is now measured against its OWN level outside the window, so a
    gap that was always there subtracts out and only a change is left

    the null redraws each group from ITS OWN latencies rather than shuffling
    labels over the pooled ones, because a slow subgroup varies more in
    milliseconds for the same reason it is slow, and pooling judged it against
    an average variation that was not its own

  The cost is power, and it was paid deliberately. Against shuffled labels the
  46.8% rung was found in 4 runs of 5 and that fixture still false-confirmed in
  1 of 5; against this null the fixture is clean in 5 of 5 and the 46.8% rung
  falls to 2 of 5. A miss costs a finding. A false confirmation costs the reason
  to believe the findings that remain, which is the trade this project has
  already said it makes.

  RISKS THIS CARRIES. Each is a way the null can be wrong, stated with what the
  corpus measured about it rather than left for a reader to discover.

  1. THE OFFSET IS A SUBTRACTION, NOT A RATIO. Each group is measured against
     its own level outside the window, and that level is subtracted in
     milliseconds, because the observation being explained is in milliseconds
     and so is the magnitude reported for it. A rise that is genuinely
     multiplicative -- everything doubles -- moves a slow subgroup by more
     absolute milliseconds than a fast one, and this probe will read that as
     concentrated. It is a different case from the fixture above, which rises
     by a fixed amount. No case in this corpus tests it, so this risk is
     stated and not measured, which is a weaker thing than the rest of this
     document and is marked as such.

  2. THE 5% IS PER DIMENSION, AND FOUR ARE TESTED. The null covers the choice
     of the most extreme value WITHIN a dimension, because it is a null of the
     maximum. It does not cover the choice among the four dimensions. A run
     therefore carries roughly a 1-(0.95)^4 = 18% chance of confirming
     something somewhere when nothing is concentrated anywhere. That is the
     source of every remaining false confirmation named in section 2, and it
     is the declared rate behaving as declared, not an anomaly.

  3. A GROUP'S SPREAD IS ITSELF ESTIMATED, FROM ONE WINDOW. Redrawing each
     group from its own latencies is what stopped the widest group being
     judged against everyone else's variation -- but the spread it redraws
     from comes from that group's documents in this window alone, around 50 in
     these fixtures. The wider a group is relative to the rest, the noisier
     both the statistic and the threshold it is compared against become. The
     fixture that exercises this runs one endpoint about six times slower than
     the others before the rise and about twice as wide after it, and is clean
     in 5 runs of 5. Nothing here bounds what happens further out than that.

  4. THE NULL IS ESTIMATED ON WHAT WAS READ. The redraws run over a capped
     sample of the focus window. Observed statistic and null come from the same
     documents, so the comparison stays valid, but on a window larger than the
     cap it is a test on that sample rather than on the window -- and the
     evidence line says so when it happens. No focus window in this corpus
     exceeded the cap. Every baseline did: the baseline is every bucket except
     the focus one, so each group's level here is established from a random
     draw of it rather than from all of it. Random rather than the first
     documents returned, because on a time-ordered index those are its oldest,
     and a normal measured from the oldest stretch of a baseline is a normal
     for a different span of time than the one being explained.

7. WHAT THIS EVALUATION CANNOT SEE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Every number above is a rate of something this harness can produce. None of
  them is a rate of what it cannot, and a zero printed in section 6 reads
  identically either way.

  Each case here is generated, indexed, audited once, and deleted. No index is
  read twice, and none is read at any moment other than immediately after it
  was written. A defect that appears *between* runs -- the same command over
  the same unchanged data returning a different answer -- therefore cannot land
  in any category above, including the empty ones.

  This is not hypothetical. The scan window was anchored on the wall clock, so
  the focus window moved between runs over a static index and the left edge cut
  the oldest bucket in half. An agent A/B run found it because it happened to
  read one index at two moments. This evaluation reported a clean sweep on both
  sides of the fix -- identical verdicts, identical rates -- because reading one
  index at two moments is the one thing it never does. See
  examples/window-anchor.txt.

  Multiple seeds per case fixed the neighbouring blindness -- a failure that
  depends on where the noise fell -- and it is worth being precise that they
  are different holes. Seeds vary the data. Nothing here varies the clock.

  Stated as work rather than as a caveat: closing this needs a case that reads
  one index more than once, at moments it chooses. The corpus has none.
